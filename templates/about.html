🧠 Understanding Brain Tumours

A brain tumour is an abnormal growth of cells inside the brain. These growths can be benign (non-cancerous) or malignant (cancerous). As the brain controls essential functions like memory, speech, vision, and movement, any tumour can affect daily life significantly.

💡 Early detection can greatly improve treatment success and survival rates.

🔬 Types of Brain Tumours

Brain tumours are categorized based on where they start and how they behave. Here are the most common types explained simply:

  • Glioma: The most common type. It begins in brain support cells and can grow slowly or aggressively.
  • Meningioma: Starts in the protective covering of the brain. Usually slow-growing and often non-cancerous.
  • Pituitary Tumour: Affects hormone-producing glands and may cause hormonal imbalance.
  • Metastatic Tumour: Cancer that spreads to the brain from other parts like lungs or breast.

📊 Tumour Stages

Tumours are classified into stages based on how aggressive they are:

  • Grade I: Slow-growing, least harmful
  • Grade II: May grow slowly but can return
  • Grade III: Malignant and faster growing
  • Grade IV: Highly aggressive (e.g., Glioblastoma)

⚠ Common Symptoms

Symptoms may vary depending on tumour location, but common signs include:

  • Frequent headaches
  • Seizures
  • Blurred vision or speech issues
  • Memory problems
  • Loss of balance or coordination

🛡 Precautions & Prevention

  • Go for regular medical checkups
  • Avoid unnecessary radiation exposure
  • Maintain a healthy lifestyle
  • Pay attention to early neurological symptoms

🥗 Recommended Diet

A healthy diet supports brain health and overall immunity:

  • Fruits & vegetables (rich in antioxidants)
  • Whole grains and nuts
  • Omega-3 foods (fish, flaxseeds)
  • Avoid processed and sugary foods

💡 Medical Recommendations

  • Consult a neurologist if symptoms appear
  • Regular MRI/CT scans when advised
  • Follow proper treatment plans strictly
